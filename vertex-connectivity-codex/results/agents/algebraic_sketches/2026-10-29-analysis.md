## A. Strengthened bounded-rank obstruction (candidate A-01)

**Theorem.** For all integers `n >= 2` and `r >= 0`, there are vertex-specific binary linear maps

\[
 A_v:\mathbb F_2^{n-1}\longrightarrow\mathbb F_2^b,
 \qquad b=r+\lceil\log_2 n\rceil+2,
\]

such that, for every nonzero symmetric zero-diagonal binary `n x n` matrix `D` of rank at most `r`, some `A_v(D_v)` is nonzero. Equivalently, every two graphs whose adjacency XOR difference is nonzero and has binary rank at most `r` have distinct concatenated transcripts under these maps.

The maps are chosen once and then deterministic. The proof establishes existence, not an efficient uniform construction. Evaluation of a chosen dense map takes `O(nb)` binary operations per vertex. This is a statement about distinguishing restricted differences, **not** a protocol for deciding connectivity. If `b > n-1`, sending the full row already suffices and the assertion is uninteresting but valid. When `r <= 1`, there are no nonzero alternating matrices of such rank, so the assertion is vacuous. The use of `r` rather than the prior note's `2r` is the new quantitative improvement in this report.

### A.1 Counting alternating matrices of bounded rank

**Lemma.** For every `t >= 1` and `r >= 2`, the number of symmetric zero-diagonal `t x t` matrices over `F_2` of rank at most `r` is at most `2^(rt)`.

**Proof.** Such a matrix is the matrix of an alternating bilinear form: `z^T D z = 0` for all `z`. If `r >= t`, count all `2^(t(t-1)/2)` alternating matrices, which is at most `2^(rt)`.

Suppose `2 <= r < t`. Every column space of dimension at most `r` is contained in at least one `r`-dimensional subspace `W` of `F_2^t`. Fix a basis matrix `U` of size `t x r` for `W` and a left inverse `L` satisfying `LU=I_r`. If an alternating `D` has column space inside `W`, then symmetry gives its row space inside the corresponding row span, and

\[
 D=U C U^T,\qquad C=L D L^T.
\]

Indeed `UL D=D` and its transpose is `D L^T U^T=D`. The `r x r` matrix `C` is alternating, so there are at most `2^(r(r-1)/2)` choices for it. This count may overcount `D`, which is harmless.

The number of `r`-subspaces is the Gaussian binomial coefficient

\[
 {t\brack r}_2
 =2^{r(t-r)}\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\frac{1-2^{i-t}}{1-2^{i-r}}
 <4\,2^{r(t-r)}.
\]

For the last bound, the numerator product is at most one and the denominator is `prod_{j=1}^r(1-2^(-j)) > 1/4`. To check the latter without an external estimate, the first two factors have product `3/8`; the remaining finite product is at least `1-sum_{j=3}^r 2^(-j) > 3/4`, hence the whole product exceeds `9/32 > 1/4` (with `r=2` handled directly).

Therefore the count is at most

\[
 4\,2^{r(t-r)}2^{r(r-1)/2}
 =4\,2^{rt-r(r+1)/2}\le 2^{rt},
\]

because `r(r+1)/2 >= 3`. This proves the lemma. The count includes zero; no assumption of nonsingularity is hidden.

### A.2 Probabilistic existence of local maps

Choose every entry of every `b x (n-1)` matrix `A_v` independently and uniformly from `F_2`. For a fixed nonzero `D` with `t` nonzero rows, each of those `t` local row vectors is nonzero, so

\[
 \Pr[A_v(D_v)=0\text{ for every }v]=2^{-bt}.
\]

The independence is across the independently chosen matrices, not across the graph rows. Symmetry of `D` means all its nonzero columns lie in its row support. Thus the number of possibilities with support of size `t` and rank at most `r` is at most `binom(n,t) 2^(rt)` by A.1. A union bound gives

\[
 \Pr[\exists D\ne0,\ \operatorname{rank}D\le r,\ A_vD_v=0\ \forall v]
 \le \sum_{t=1}^n\binom nt2^{(r-b)t}
 \le \sum_{t=1}^n2^{(\log_2 n+r-b)t}
 \le \sum_{t=1}^{\infty}4^{-t}=\frac13<1.
\]

A successful deterministic choice therefore exists. As noted, `r=0,1` are vacuous and require no counting argument.

### A.3 Consequences and limits

- The theorem implies a collision proof for all arbitrary encoders cannot restrict its witnesses to rank `r` when the allowed local communication is at least `r + ceil(log_2 n) + 2`.
- Rank-two switches, including `ab^T + ba^T` and biclique adjacency differences, can all be excluded by only `ceil(log_2 n)+4` bits per vertex.
- For an asymptotic target with `b >> log n`, this improves the prior counting barrier from rank about `b/2` to rank about `b`.
- These sketches still fail exact connectivity whenever total output rank is less than `binom(n,2)`; see C.1. Their surviving kernel directions simply have larger adjacency rank.
- The theorem does **not** disprove the unrestricted symmetric-collision conjecture, which explicitly allows high-rank `D`.

## B. Complete-graph two-lifts and exact scope of the reduction

For `m >= 3`, a binary labeling `x` of `E(K_m)` defines a two-sheeted lift with vertices `(v,b)`, `v in [m]`, `b in F_2`, and edges `(u,b)--(v,b+x_uv)`. The base edge universe is `E(K_m)` and the lifted graph has the ordinary fixed ambient edge universe `E(K_(2m))`; only a restricted family of those lifted graphs is being considered. No contraction or sketch dimension change is involved.

Let `Cut_m={delta s : s in F_2^m}`, with `(delta s)_uv=s_u+s_v`.

**Lift dichotomy.** If `x=delta s`, each edge preserves `b+s_v`, so the two level sets form two disjoint `m`-cliques. If `x` is not a cut, some triangle has odd label sum: if every root triangle had even sum, setting `s_1=0`, `s_v=x_1v` would express `x=delta s`. The lift of an odd triangle joins both copies of a base vertex; paths in the connected base graph then reach both copies of every base vertex. The lift is consequently connected. It is always `(m-1)`-regular.

**Communication reduction.** A player given only base star `x_v` can reconstruct the exact neighborhoods of both `(v,0)` and `(v,1)`. For fixed-length messages, concatenating their two messages simulates any deterministic connectivity protocol on `2m` vertices. Negate its Boolean output to recognize `Cut_m`. Hence, for fixed-length or log-alphabet communication complexity,

\[
 D_{\mathrm{conn}}(2m)\ge\tfrac12D_{\mathrm{cut}}(m).
\]

This holds for arbitrary vertex-specific encoders and arbitrary referees. If each original message has fixed length at most `B` (known for each vertex), the simulated base message has length at most `2B`. If messages are variable binary strings of length at most `B`, raw concatenation is not uniquely parseable. Instead injectively encode each possible local string into `B+1` fixed bits (there are fewer than `2^(B+1)` such strings), giving `D_cut,fixed(m) <= 2(B+1)`. The asymptotic reduction is unchanged. No party acquires another player's neighborhood.

### B.1 Candidate-coordinate formulation

Gauge `s_1=0` and set `x_vv=0`. Define `P_v(x)_i=x_vi+x_v1`. This is a bijection from the local star to the `m-1`-dimensional set `S={s:s_1=0}`: for `v != 1`, its `v` coordinate gives `x_v1`, and then every star entry is recovered; for `v=1`, the map is the star itself. For a cut `delta s`, every `P_v` equals `s`.

Thus arbitrary local messages are arbitrary `h_v(P_v(x))`. If a symmetric zero-diagonal nonzero `D` with root row zero satisfies `h_v(s+D_v)=h_v(s)` for every `v`, then `x=delta s+D` is a noncut colliding with the cut `delta s`. A cut with zero root row is zero, which is the essential final implication.

The root-row restriction is sufficient but not necessary. Removing it requires handling the correction `P_v(delta s+D)=s+D_v+D_v1*1` in candidate coordinates, rather than silently retaining `s+D_v`.

**Open:** I have neither proved this simultaneous high-rank collision assertion for arbitrary `h_v` with sublinear `b`, nor constructed a sublinear family avoiding every such collision. Local fiber cardinalities alone are insufficient: the symmetric consistency constraint is a real additional condition.

## C. Restricted lower bounds audited algebraically

### C.1 Arbitrary local XOR-affine encoders for connectivity

If the concatenated sketch is `A x+c`, with `x in F_2^(binom(n,2))`, exact connectivity implies that `A` is injective. Suppose a nonzero kernel vector has edge support `D`; choose any `e in D` and a spanning tree `T` containing `e`. Let `G_0=T\D`, `G_1=(T\D) union D`. Their XOR difference is `D`; `G_0` is a proper subgraph of a tree and disconnected, whereas `G_1` contains `T` and is connected. Their transcripts would agree. Thus

\[
 \sum_v B_v\ge\binom n2,\qquad B\ge\lceil(n-1)/2\rceil.
\]

Balanced edge ownership attains the maximum-message bound: orient `K_n` so every outdegree is at most `ceil((n-1)/2)` and have a vertex transmit the edge bits it owns. For odd `n`, the cyclic tournament gives equal outdegree; for even `n`, delete a vertex from such a tournament on `n+1` vertices. The referee reconstructs the graph.

This is not a nonlinear lower bound. Its decisive property is that a kernel collision is invariant under arbitrary common translations of graph inputs. A general message collision has no such property.

Exactly XOR-mergeable messages also have this translation-invariance: if `M(S)=M(T)`, merging both with `M(U)` yields `M(S triangle U)=M(T triangle U)`. Fibers are cosets of the empty-set fiber, which is a subgroup. Relabeling fibers gives binary quotient-linear maps and the same lower bound when each alphabet has at most `2^B_v` symbols. For arbitrary variable strings of maximum length `B_v`, replace `B_v` by `B_v+1` in this counting statement. This argument requires exact merging for all pairs; merely additive update semantics over an odd-characteristic field do not establish it.

### C.2 Local XOR-linear recognition of the cut space

**Theorem.** For `m >= 3`, a correct cut-space recognizer whose local star messages are binary affine-linear has concatenated linear rank at least `binom(m,2)-1`. Consequently fixed-length messages satisfy `sum_v B_v >= binom(m,2)-1` and `B >= ceil((binom(m,2)-1)/m)`.

**Proof.** Subtract affine constants and let `K` be the global kernel. Correctness implies `K subseteq Cut_m`, because a noncut in `K` would collide with the zero cut. If `dim K >= 2`, take independent cuts `delta a, delta b` in `K`, with gauges `a_1=b_1=0`. Put

\[
 D_{uv}=a_u b_v+b_u a_v.
\]

At vertex `v`, its incident row is `D_v=a_v(delta b)_v+b_v(delta a)_v`, so the local linear map kills it. Hence `D in K`. The matrix is symmetric, zero-diagonal, and has zero root row. It is nonzero because independent `a,b` have two coordinates with independent coordinate-pairs, whose determinant is one. Therefore `D` is not a cut, a contradiction. Thus `dim K <= 1`, proving the rank bound.

The rank bound itself is sharp: choose kernel spanned by the cut consisting of a fixed root's entire star. Each nonroot vertex can send all nonroot incident edge bits; the root sends the `m-2` differences `x_12+x_1j` for `j=3,...,m`. The global kernel is exactly that one-dimensional cut space and thus membership in `Cut_m` is constant on each transcript fiber. This construction proves rank sharpness, not optimal maximum per-vertex communication for every `m`.

### C.3 Common nonlinear candidate fingerprints

If all base players use a single arbitrary `h:S -> {0,1}^b`, every fiber of `h` must avoid a full affine plane. For if the fiber contains `s,s+a,s+b,s+a+b` with `a,b` independent, the matrix `D=ab^T+ba^T` from C.2 gives every candidate `s+D_v` among those four points and yields a cut/noncut collision.

An affine-plane-free `A subseteq F_2^d`, `d=m-1`, satisfies `binom(|A|,2) <= 2^d-1`: distinct unordered pairs cannot have equal nonzero sum, since then their four distinct points form an affine plane. Consequently

\[
 2^b\ge\frac{2^{m-1}}{(1+\sqrt{8\cdot2^{m-1}-7})/2},
 \qquad b\ge (m-1)/2-O(1).
\]

For at most `q` distinct maps, apply the argument to their joint map to obtain `q b >= (m-1)/2-O(1)`. This does not cover arbitrary vertex-specific maps with an unbounded number of types. A.3 explains why simply reusing rank-two witnesses cannot overcome that restriction.

## D. Independent exploratory attempts and exact obstructions

1. **Cut transcript fibers.** Even without common maps, a set of cut parameters giving the same *entire transcript* must be affine-plane-free, by C.3 with the joint map `(h_1,...,h_m)`. This only gives total output about `m/2` bits and therefore a constant maximum-message bound. Charging the full `Theta(m^2)` edge input size to the cut transcript is unjustified: the cut space itself has only `m-1` dimensions.

2. **Local collisions transported to another graph.** The tree-overlay method in C.1 cannot be extended from linear maps by observing that some arbitrary inputs collide. The base point of a nonlinear collision matters. A proof would need a normal-form or translation-invariance theorem that is presently absent.

3. **Degree / weighted-sum encoders on candidate rows.** Equal unweighted sums force balanced row changes relative to the cut parameter; they do not force `D=0`, since balanced exchanges can survive. Vertex-dependent small linear checks can exclude all small-rank versions at once (A.3). No argument here rules out the high-rank balanced exchanges required by the real target.

4. **Rank decomposition into rank-two terms.** Every alternating binary `D` can be written as a sum of rank-two terms. For nonlinear `h_v`, a collision for the sum does not follow from separate failures or successes for its summands; for linear maps, proving each summand killed is much stronger than proving their sum killed. This decomposition is not a way around the bounded-rank barrier.

5. **Existential random maps.** The independence used in A.2 is legitimate because the *maps* are independently sampled and `D` is fixed before the probability calculation. It cannot be repurposed to claim independence of local message collisions for a randomly sampled symmetric graph under fixed nonlinear maps.
