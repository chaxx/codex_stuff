## D01: exact theorem and complete audit

For every integer `n>=2`, let each vertex know only its neighborhood in a simple labeled graph and send one deterministic simultaneous message from an alphabet of size at most `2^b`, where `b>=1` is an integer. If an arbitrary referee decides connectivity exactly on every graph, then

`b > n/[768(ceil(sqrt(n))+1)] > sqrt(n)/2304`.

Consequently fixed-length maximum `B` bits satisfies `B>sqrt(n)/2304`, and arbitrary binary strings of maximum length `B` satisfy `B>sqrt(n)/4608`. No randomness or running-time assumption enters. The output is one connectivity bit, not recovery or a partition.

The complete proposed proof is at lower-bounds report lines 23–114. The following checks reconstruct every substantive inference.

### Parameter split and integer boundaries

Set `q=128(ceil(sqrt(n))+1)`, `M=n-q`. If `b>sqrt(n)`, the theorem is immediate, since its first threshold is smaller than `sqrt(n)`. Otherwise `b<=sqrt(n)`.

If `M<4(qb+1)`, then

`n<(4b+1)q+4 <= (4b+2)q <= 6bq`,

using `q>=4` and `b>=1`. This gives the strict desired bound. Thus the only remaining case is `M>=4(qb+1)`. In this case `M>0` and `q<n`, so the proposed graph has exactly the available number of vertices. No reservoir is constructed in the small-`n` cases where `q>=n`. All parameters used as set cardinalities are integers; `d=16(b+1)` is even and obeys

`d <= 16(ceil(sqrt(n))+1)=q/8`.

### Four-symbol counting lemma

The statement and proof at lines 37–59 are valid. For last-coordinate slices `S_1,...,S_4`, write `U` for their union and `I` for their intersection. Each projected word contributes at most three occurrences unless in `I`, yielding `|S|<=3|U|+|I|`. If `S` does not shatter `d` coordinates, then `U` does not shatter `d`, and `I` does not shatter `d-1`: a shattered `(d-1)`-set in the intersection would permit every last-coordinate value for every such pattern. Induction and Pascal's identity give

`|S| <= sum_(j=0)^(d-1) binom(q,j) 3^(q-j)`.

The boundary conventions `F(q,0)=0` and `F(q,d)=4^q` for `d>q` are correct, including `q=0`. No unproved bibliographic invocation is needed for the counting inequality.

### Local witness and its monotonicity under extension

At lines 61–77, each actual center encoder is restricted to neighborhoods contained in the fixed reservoir. This is a legitimate restriction of the original encoder, with every center-center edge absent.

On a `d`-set `Y`, fix an included vertex `alpha` and excluded partner `beta`, then choose one vertex from every other pair. There are `2^(d/2-1)=2^(8b+7)>2^(2b)` choices, and at most `2^(2b)` ordered pairs `(f(A),f(Y\A))`. Thus two distinct choices `A_0,A_1` have the same ordered message pair. The four sets

`P^0=A_0 intersection A_1`, `P^1=Y\(A_0 union A_1)`, `R^0=A_0\A_1`, `R^1=A_1\A_0`

are disjoint and partition `Y`. The first contains `alpha`, the second contains `beta`, and the last two are both nonempty because distinct equal-cardinality pair choices differ in both directions. The two message equalities are exactly the equalities needed for the two possible `P` choices. Labeling these witnesses fixes a partial four-label assignment whose every extension is good: no witness vertex or its set membership disappears when the remaining vertices receive labels. This last universal-over-extensions property is what permits the shattering argument.

### One partition good for every encoder

For each center, the bad-labeling family misses at least one pattern on every `d`-coordinate set, by the preceding witness construction. Therefore its uniform measure is bounded by `Pr[Z<d]` for `Z~Bin(q,1/4)`. Since `d<=q/8` and `mu=q/4`, exponential Markov at `t=ln 2` gives

`Pr[Z<=mu/2] <= exp(mu[(ln 2)/2-1/2]) <= exp(-q/32)`.

The inequality `(ln 2)/2-1/2 < -1/8` has the correct sign. The union bound over at most `n` centers is at most `n exp(-4 sqrt(n))<1`. No independence of bad events across centers is asserted or required. The randomness here is a proof tool for selecting an adversarial partition; the protocol remains entirely deterministic.

### All vertices' messages and graph connectivity

Fix the partition and every witness before selecting bit strings. Let `G(a,z)` have cliques `P_0 union R_0` and `P_1 union R_1`, and center `i` adjacent precisely to `P_i^(a_i) union R_i^(z_i)`.

The complete dependency calculation is:

| Vertex class | Its actual neighborhood | Message dependence |
|---|---|---|
| Center `i` | The two selected witness sets | Only `a_i`, by the two proved local equalities |
| A vertex in `P_0 union P_1` | Its fixed reservoir clique and centers whose selected `P`-set contains it | Only `a` |
| A vertex in `R_0 union R_1` | Its fixed reservoir clique and centers whose selected `R`-set contains it | Only `z` |

The last class has at most `q` vertices. Its joint transcript therefore has at most `2^(qb)` possibilities. Because `M>=4(qb+1)>qb`, distinct `x,y` have equal `R` transcripts. Compare `G(x,x)` and `G(x,y)`: their centers and `P` vertices agree because `a=x` is fixed; their `R` vertices agree by the transcript collision. This compares every player's message and respects symmetry of all changed edges.

Every witness set is nonempty, so both reservoir cliques are nonempty. In `G(x,x)`, each center attaches only to its one clique, giving exactly two components. In `G(x,y)`, any differing coordinate supplies a center adjacent to both cliques; every other center has a clique neighbor. Thus this graph is connected. The full transcript collision contradicts exact Boolean decision, without assuming recovery or shared knowledge of the partition.

The contradiction eliminates `M>=4(qb+1)`, so the parameter split gives `b>n/(6q)` as claimed.

### Strict constants and bit conventions

For `n>=2`, `ceil(sqrt(n))+1<=sqrt(n)+2<3sqrt(n)`, giving the second strict inequality. Zero-bit protocols are impossible because empty and complete graphs have different answers. Fixed-length budget `B` therefore has `B>=1`, and the alphabet theorem applies with `b=B`. For strings of length at most `B`, the alphabet has size `2^(B+1)-1<2^(B+1)`. Apply with `b=B+1` and use `B+1<=2B` to obtain the factor-two weaker stated constant. No prefix-free convention is silently imposed.

**Final D01 verdict: VERIFIED.** The unrestricted `Omega(sqrt(n))` baseline is fully supported. No improved unrestricted exponent or near-linear lower bound follows.

## Selected coefficient-r barrier

The theorem at algebraic report lines 9–18, with `b=r+ceil(log_2 n)+2`, matches the independently proved statement in the blind report.

The proponent's counting lemma at lines 20–51 is valid. For `2<=r<t`, every column space is contained in an `r`-subspace `W`; selecting a basis matrix `U` and a left inverse `L` gives `D=U(LDL^T)U^T`. Symmetry is used to justify the right multiplication, and `LDL^T` remains alternating. The Gaussian-binomial estimate is sound: the denominator product exceeds `1/4`, and multiplication by the `2^(r(r-1)/2)` possible alternating forms gives at most `2^(rt)`. The separate case `r>=t` is also valid. Ranks zero and one are vacuous for a nonzero alternating matrix.

The proof at lines 53–70 correctly groups matrices by their **actual** active vertex set. For each fixed `D` with `t` nonzero rows, independently random local maps annihilate it with probability exactly `2^(-bt)`. Graph symmetry does not compromise independence of the independently sampled maps. Counting extra matrices of smaller support in the upper bound is harmless; they are not assigned an unjustified actual annihilation probability. The union bound is bounded by `sum_(t>=1)4^(-t)=1/3<1`.

The blind report supplies a second complete counting proof using the symplectic normal form `D=U J U^T`; thus the selected result has both a blind independent derivation and an adversarial check of the proposed Gaussian-binomial proof.

**Final selected-barrier verdict: VERIFIED.** This is an existence theorem for fixed deterministic maps, with no assertion of efficient uniform construction. It refutes a universal collision lemma that, using only the bit budget, would demand a nonzero bounded-rank collision for every encoder collection. It does not itself refute a collision argument using additional consequences of an assumed correct connectivity protocol, and does not supply a correct short connectivity protocol.

## Cut-space rank lower bound

Algebraic C.2, lines 124–134, is valid and agrees with the blind independent proof. Subtracting affine constants is legitimate. Correctness forces the global kernel into the cut space because zero is a cut. Two independent kernel cuts, gauged to zero at a fixed root, produce `D_uv=a_u b_v+b_u a_v`. Each star is a linear combination of the two killed local cut stars, so all local maps kill `D`. Independence supplies a nonzero two-by-two minor, while the root row is zero; a cut with zero root row must be zero. Thus this nonzero `D` cannot be a cut, contradiction. Rank-nullity gives `rank L>=binom(m,2)-1`.

The sharpness construction also checks out: all nonroot-to-nonroot edges are forced to zero in the kernel, and the root's difference checks force all root edges to one common bit. The kernel is exactly the span of the root-star cut. Since that kernel is a cut subspace, membership in cut space is constant on every transcript fiber. This proves tightness of the rank bound; it does not prove an optimal maximum-message bound for every `m`.

**Verdict: VERIFIED**, including vertex-specific affine maps and an arbitrary referee. No conclusion about arbitrary nonlinear encoders is warranted.

## Lift statements and the anchored counterexample

The public-lift reconstruction theorem at lower-bounds report lines 132–140 agrees with the blind protocol: each lifted vertex lists the sheet index of its unique neighbor in each publicly ordered neighboring fiber. The maximum length is `Delta(H) ceil(log_2 r)`, and the referee reconstructs the graph in polynomial time. Public base/fiber labeling and the matching promise are essential to this precise statement.

The following asymptotic wording should be tightened if reused. To infer from `O((n/r) log r)` that the family cannot establish an `Omega(n/log^C n)` lower bound, a sufficient condition is

`r/log r = omega(log^C n)`.

Merely saying that `r` grows faster than `log^C n` omits the numerator's `log r` factor. The report's concrete `r>=sqrt(n)` ceiling of `O(sqrt(n) log n)` is valid.

The complete-graph two-lift dichotomy at algebraic report lines 82–86 is valid: cuts give two cliques, and a noncut has an odd triangle whose lift connects both sheets, after which base connectivity reaches every lifted vertex. The simulation at lines 88–94 is local and preserves arbitrary encoders. Fixed-length or log-alphabet cost doubles. Variable strings need parsing; encoding each original string in `B+1` fixed bits makes the stated `2(B+1)` bound correct.

The candidate map and zero-root sufficient collision criterion at lines 98–102 are valid. In particular, dropping the anchor requires the correction `D_v1 * 1`; it cannot be silently omitted. The independently checked four-vertex example in the blind report shows why absence of this specific anchored witness does not imply cut-space recognition. It is not a counterexample to the asymptotic unrestricted target.
