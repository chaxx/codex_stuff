## Exact baseline statement submitted for independent audit

**Theorem D-01(k=1).** Let n≥2 be an integer. Vertex v∈[n] knows only its neighborhood in a labeled simple undirected graph, and sends one simultaneous deterministic message. Suppose each message alphabet has size at most 2^b, for an integer b≥1. If an arbitrary referee decides connectivity correctly on every graph, then

\[
 b>\frac{n}{768(\lceil\sqrt n\rceil+1)} > \frac{\sqrt n}{2304}.
\]

The second strict inequality in the display is valid for n≥2 because \(\lceil\sqrt n\rceil+1<3\sqrt n\). In particular fixed-length B-bit messages have B>√n/2304. For variable strings of length at most B, including the empty string as an available symbol, B>√n/4608. The output is only one connectivity bit; the theorem is not a graph-recovery theorem. Success is exact, and there is no randomness or runtime bound.

This statement and its scope were sent separately from the proof to the coordinator for a fresh auditor's first pass. The present specialist's conclusion is a **complete self-contained proof, pending independent audit**, not independent verification.

## Full proof

Write q=128(⌈√n⌉+1) and M=n−q. If b>√n the asserted bound already follows, so assume b≤√n. If

\[
 M<4(qb+1), \tag{1}
\]

then

\[
 n<(4b+1)q+4\le (4b+2)q\le 6bq,
\]

using q≥4 and b≥1, proving the result. Thus it remains to rule out M≥4(qb+1). This hypothesis implies q<n, so a q-vertex reservoir U and M distinct center vertices exist. No construction is attempted when q≥n; that case was already handled by (1).

### Lemma A: a four-symbol shattering bound

Let S⊆[4]^q. If no coordinate set of size d is fully shattered (that is, for every such set at least one of its 4^d assignments is missing from the projection), then

\[
 |S|\le F(q,d):=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\binom qj 3^{q-j}.
\]

Use F(q,0)=0 and F(q,d)=4^q when d>q. This includes the empty-family boundary d=0: any nonempty family shatters the empty coordinate set.

**Proof.** Induct on q, with q=0 immediate. For a∈[4], let S_a be the last-coordinate-a slice, projected onto [q−1]. Put U=⋃_a S_a and I=⋂_a S_a. A projected word occurs in at most three slices unless it belongs to I, so

\[
 |S|=\sum_a|S_a|\le3|U|+|I|.
\]

U cannot shatter d coordinates, since any pattern witnessed in U is also the restriction of a word of S. I cannot shatter d−1 coordinates: adjoining the last coordinate would then give all four values for every projected pattern and shatter d coordinates in S. Induction and Pascal's identity give

\[
 |S|\le3F(q-1,d)+F(q-1,d-1)=F(q,d).
\]

The cases d>q and d=0 are already boundary cases. This proves the lemma. It is the special four-symbol Karpovsky–Milman/Sauer bound needed here, with its entire proof included rather than assumed from a citation. ∎

### Lemma B: a local four-part witness

Put d=16(b+1). For each center c_i with message function f_i:2^U→[2^b], and every d-element subset Y⊆U, some labeling of Y by P_0,P_1,R_0,R_1 forces the following property in every extension to U: there are nonempty sets P_i^a⊆P_a and R_i^a⊆R_a (a=0,1) such that

\[
 f_i(P_i^0\cup R_i^0)=f_i(P_i^0\cup R_i^1), \qquad
 f_i(P_i^1\cup R_i^0)=f_i(P_i^1\cup R_i^1). \tag{2}
\]

**Proof.** Pair the d vertices of Y. Designate one pair {α,β}. Consider sets A containing α, not β, and exactly one vertex of each remaining pair. There are 2^(d/2−1)=2^(8b+7)>2^(2b) such sets, whereas the ordered message pair (f_i(A),f_i(Y∖A)) has at most 2^(2b) values. Choose distinct A_0,A_1 with the same ordered pair. Define

\[
 P_i^0=A_0\cap A_1,\quad P_i^1=Y\setminus(A_0\cup A_1),\quad
 R_i^0=A_0\setminus A_1,\quad R_i^1=A_1\setminus A_0.
\]

The first two sets contain α and β respectively. The last two sets are both nonempty, since distinct choices of one vertex per pair differ on a whole pair. These four sets partition Y. The equality of first messages gives the first equation in (2); the equality of complementary messages gives the second. Label their vertices accordingly. Extending the labeling elsewhere does not remove the four witnessing subsets. ∎

### Lemma C: one partition works for all centers

There is a partition U=P_0⊔P_1⊔R_0⊔R_1 satisfying Lemma B for every center.

**Proof.** Call a labeling bad for c_i if no witness exists. By Lemma B, its bad family does not shatter any d-coordinate set. Lemma A therefore bounds its uniform probability by

\[
 4^{-q}\sum_{j<d}\binom qj3^{q-j}
 =\Pr[Z<d],\qquad Z\sim\operatorname{Bin}(q,1/4).
\]

The assumptions b≤√n and the definitions give d≤q/8. Write μ=q/4. For completeness, exponential Markov with t=ln 2 gives

\[
 \Pr[Z\le\mu/2]
 \le e^{t\mu/2}\mathbb E e^{-tZ}
 =e^{t\mu/2}(1-\tfrac14+\tfrac14e^{-t})^q
 \le \exp\{\mu(t/2-1/2)\}
 \le e^{-\mu/8}=e^{-q/32},
\]

where 1+x≤e^x and (ln 2)/2−1/2<−1/8 were used. A union bound over M≤n centers gives at most ne^(−q/32)≤ne^(−4√n)<1. The last inequality holds for all n≥1: with x=√n≥1, 2ln x−4x<0. Thus some partition is good for all centers. No independence between different centers' bad events is used. ∎

### Lemma D: the global transcript collision

Fix the common partition and four witnessing sets for each center. For a,b'∈{0,1}^M construct G(a,b') as follows:

- P_0∪R_0 and P_1∪R_1 induce cliques, with no edge between these two reservoir parts.
- Center c_i has exactly the neighborhood P_i^(a_i)∪R_i^(b'_i).
- There are no center-center edges.

All four witnesses are nonempty. Hence G(x,x) has exactly two components. If x≠y, a center at a differing coordinate connects the two cliques in G(x,y), and every other center has a neighbor in a clique, so G(x,y) is connected.

By (2), the center c_i's message depends on a_i but not b'_i. A P vertex sees its fixed clique and centers selected only by a, so its message depends only on a. An R vertex sees its fixed clique and centers selected only by b', so its message depends only on b'. Thus the whole R-vertex transcript is a function T(b') with at most 2^(qb) values. Since M≥4(qb+1)>qb, there exist distinct x,y with T(x)=T(y). Comparing G(x,x) and G(x,y), centers and P vertices agree because a=x is fixed, and R vertices agree by the choice of x,y. The full transcripts coincide despite opposite connectivity. This contradicts correctness.

Consequently (1) must hold and b>n/(6q), proving the alphabet statement. Fixed-length B uses b=B; zero-bit protocols are impossible because empty and complete graphs have opposite answers. For variable length at most B, the number of possible strings is 1+2+⋯+2^B=2^(B+1)−1≤2^(B+1), so apply the theorem with b=B+1. Again B≥1, hence B+1≤2B, and B>√n/4608 follows. ∎

## Adversarial checks on the baseline

1. **Small n / reservoir larger than the graph.** If q≥n then M≤0, so (1) is automatic and n<6bq is valid. The proof never instantiates a nonexistent reservoir.
2. **No assumed shared neighborhoods.** f_i is the actual arbitrary local encoder on a subset of the fixed reservoir; every other vertex's dependency is computed from its actual neighborhood. The partition is chosen by the adversary for this fixed protocol, not communicated to players as extra information.
3. **Nonlinear and vertex-specific messages.** The only property used is each actual message alphabet bound. No linearity, merging, continuity, low-degree, or common-map assumption enters.
4. **Symmetric edge consistency.** The edge set is defined once, through center-reservoir adjacencies and two cliques, so both endpoints see consistent inputs.
5. **Output strength.** Only connected versus disconnected inputs are compared. No recovery/injectivity requirement is imposed.
6. **All-k scope.** The proof above specializes to k=1 and uses distinctness of x,y. It provides no independent verdict on the stronger all-k claims elsewhere in the draft or notes.
7. **Bibliographic scope.** Lemma A is self-contained, so no inaccessible theorem is needed for correctness; priority of this derivation and the benchmark is left to the literature specialist.

## Independent attempts beyond the two-clique reservoir

### Attempt 1: many-sheet graph coverings — an exact upper-bound obstruction

The motivation was to amplify the two-sheet lift into an r-sheet lift with many independently switchable layers, hoping that connectedness of the cover would force much higher global consistency than the reservoir construction.

**Proposition (public-cover description ceiling).** Fix a connected simple base graph H on m publicly labeled vertices and an integer r≥2. Label the n=mr vertices of a lift by (v,a)∈V(H)×[r]. Promise that every base edge uv is replaced by a perfect matching between its r-vertex fibers and there are no other edges. On this promise family, a deterministic local simultaneous protocol exactly reconstructs the graph, and hence decides connectivity, using at most

\[
 \Delta(H)\lceil\log_2r\rceil
\]

bits per vertex and polynomial decoding time. It succeeds with certainty for every choice of edge matchings.

**Proof.** Vertex (v,a) has exactly one neighbor in each public fiber u adjacent to v in H. In the fixed public order of N_H(v), send each neighbor's sheet index in ⌈log₂r⌉ bits. The referee reconstructs every incident edge from the base label plus sheet index, then uses graph search. No private knowledge beyond the local neighborhood is needed. ∎

For H=K_m this is (m−1)⌈log₂r⌉=O((n/r)log r). Therefore increasing the number of sheets to r≥√n makes this family decidable in O(√n log n) bits per vertex; more precisely, for any fixed C≥0, this upper bound rules out an Ω(n/log^C n) lower bound on the family whenever r/log r=ω(log^C n). A many-sheet strategy can still be useful for other goals, but a proof demanding a larger lower bound on this fixed-base family is false. Sparse bases make the ceiling still lower. This is a barrier for a concrete attempted family, not an upper bound for arbitrary graphs.

This ceiling relies on the fixed public base/fiber labeling that defines the attempted hard family. A construction with unknown/adversarial fiber labels is a different family and is not covered without a new encoding argument.

### Attempt 2: cycles of dense clusters / partition-lattice fooling families

A natural alternative is to hide a component partition in a dense union of cliques, or to connect such components in a sparse cycle. The first family alone is too weak: on the promise of a disjoint union of cliques, every vertex sends the single bit [degree=n−1]; the graph is connected iff all such bits are 1 (equivalently any one is 1). For the complementary family of complete multipartite graphs, a single bit [degree>0] per vertex similarly suffices: the graph is connected iff it is not the empty graph.

Introducing sparse links can avoid these trivial tests, but if the clusters are fixed publicly and all variable links form a bounded-degree quotient with explicitly describable endpoints, sending the local variable-edge description gives a small protocol. The precise missing ingredient is a family in which the component partition is not locally reconstructible from a cheap public template, yet every proposed collision respects both endpoints of each changed edge. Merely counting many partitions is insufficient: transcripts need not reveal the partition to decide one bit.

I did not obtain an all-vertex transcript collision for hidden, densely interwoven clusters. No claim of a new unrestricted lower bound follows from this attempt.

### Attempt 3: replace the large reservoir by uniformly tiny switches

One can try to keep most local neighborhoods fixed and hide each center's change inside a very small varying support R, hoping that the final transcript lives on far fewer than Θ(b) vertices. The following exact coding obstruction must be addressed.

**Proposition (local small-change separation).** Let L≥1 and d≥1 be integers, and put

\[
 V=\sum_{j=1}^{\min(d-1,L)}\binom Lj.
\]

If V=0, the zero-bit map has the property below. Otherwise there exists an F₂-linear map A:F₂^L→F₂^t with t=⌈log₂(V+1)⌉ such that A(x)≠A(y) whenever 1≤|x−y|<d.

**Proof.** For a uniformly random t×L binary matrix, each fixed nonzero vector z has Az=0 with probability 2^(−t). At most V nonzero vectors have weight <d. The union-bound failure probability is V/2^t<1, proving existence. The characteristic and subtraction here are explicitly F₂; Hamming weight is ordinary binary support size. ∎

Thus encoders with only O(d log(L+1)) bits can exclude every proposed nonzero local switch of weight <d, simultaneously for all neighborhoods. Giving each vertex such a map in its own canonical incident-edge order excludes every graph-transcript collision whose changed-edge graph has a nonzero vertex degree <d. These maps need not decide connectivity and are not presented as an upper bound for it. They refute the hoped-for universal small-switch lemma, not the main lower-bound target. A successful unrestricted argument must permit denser local changes or exploit a different mechanism. This is a standard probabilistic coding argument; novelty is not asserted.

## Findings requiring follow-up

- **Established by this specialist, awaiting separate audit:** the exact k=1 baseline above; the public-cover description ceiling; the local small-change separator.
- **Not established:** any unrestricted improvement beyond Ω(√n), any Ω(n/polylog n) bound, or any reduction from arbitrary nonlinear encoders to additive encoders.
- **Concrete next target:** constant-sheet dense lifts or hidden dense partitions that avoid the public-cover ceiling, with a genuine global consistency lemma rather than independent large local fibers.
